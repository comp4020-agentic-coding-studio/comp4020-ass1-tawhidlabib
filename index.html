How these odds are calculated — no secrets

The formula: a country's chance of being picked is its estimated annual live births divided by the world's estimated annual live births, where annual births = population × crude birth rate ÷ 1,000. Every figure comes from the World Bank Open Data API (CC BY‑4.0), and each one is shown with the year it was actually reported for that country — figures land in different years because countries report on different schedules, not because any were cherry-picked. The globe's own spin is calculated too: it always turns forward to the exact longitude of the country you drew, never a faked snap-to-position. Tap any dot on the skyline above to see a stat's exact indicator and year.

"Chance of surviving your first year", "childbirth risk", and "health risks adults here face" are honest proxies built from infant mortality, maternal mortality, and adult mortality data — not literal measurements of any individual's parents. Countries without population or birth-rate data are left out of the lottery pool entirely rather than guessed at; a missing stat for an included country shows as "not reported" on its dot, never invented. Each stat's "world average" marker is this page's own population-weighted average across the lottery pool — weighted the same way the odds themselves are, not a World Bank global aggregate figure.
